01. Data Engineering

Handling sparse medical records and engineering temporal features.

The Challenge of Medical Data

Patient encounter data is inherently messy. Our dataset included up to 25 sparse diagnosis and procedure codes per patient.

  • Cascading Imputation: Missing ICD-10 and procedure codes were systematically zero-filled to maintain data structure.
  • Temporal Engineering: Calculated Days Admitted from admission and discharge dates, flagging stays over 7 days as Long_Stay.
  • Clinical Categorization: Mapped granular DRG and ICD codes to 29 broad clinical categories (e.g., CIRCULATORY, RESPIRATORY) to reduce dimensionality.

Architectural decisions made to handle clinical data constraints securely and optimally.

Domain-Specific Parsing

Rather than blindly applying generic string encoding to the primary diagnosis (AD_DGNS), a custom parsing engine was built. By stripping the first character and mapping alphanumeric structures, ICD-10 codes were algorithmically bucketed into 23 high-level clinical categories (e.g., C-series → NEOPLASMS), retaining strict medical domain significance.

O(1)

Dimensionality Constraint

With up to 50 sparse columns per row for historical diagnoses and procedures, One-Hot Encoding would have exploded the feature space into the tens of thousands. collections.Counter was utilized across all sparse columns to strictly isolate and encode only the Top 200 most frequent signals, preserving RAM and preventing the curse of dimensionality.

🔒

Data Leakage Prevention

To ensure absolutely no look-ahead bias, all category encoders (including DRG and ICD categorizers) were instantiated with handle_unknown='ignore' and fit strictly on the training subset. Test data permutations were safely routed through the frozen transformer states.

🎯

The Accuracy Paradox

In highly imbalanced readmission data, a model predicting "No Readmission" universally will still achieve ~80% accuracy. The pipeline explicitly evaluates against this paradox by utilizing ROC AUC and analyzing precision/recall trade-offs via ConfusionMatrixDisplay, alongside testing ComplementNB, which is mathematically suited for imbalanced data.

03. Modeling Pipeline

Evaluating multiple architectures against severe class imbalance.

Tree-Based Ensembles

XGBoost & LightGBM: Handled the high-dimensional, sparse data exceptionally well, but required heavy tuning to prevent overfitting on the majority class.

★ WINNING MODEL

Random Forest

The Winner: Using 200 estimators and entropy criterion, RF provided the most stable balance between accuracy and interpretability without aggressive overfitting.

Naive Bayes Variants

ComplementNB: Evaluated specifically to tackle the class imbalance, as it is designed for imbalanced datasets, though it ultimately fell short of tree-based methods.

04. Results & Evaluation

The struggle with recall in highly imbalanced medical datasets.

Performance vs. Reality

While absolute accuracy remained high across the board, the true test was the ROC AUC and Recall on the minority class (actual readmissions).

Random Forest ROC AUC 0.68+

The confusion matrices revealed that while tree-based models successfully identified true negatives, predicting true positives (readmissions) remains a significant challenge due to the lack of strong signal in the billing-oriented ICD codes.
